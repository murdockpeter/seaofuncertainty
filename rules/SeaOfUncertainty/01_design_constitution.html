Design Constitution

The rules that govern the rules. This document protects the intended player experience while the system grows in detail.

Theory should shape the pressure around the player's decisions — never replace the player's decisions.

1. Agency Before Procedure

Every major rules section must create a decision. If a player is only maintaining markers, processing arithmetic, or consulting a table without choosing among meaningful alternatives, compress or automate the procedure.

2. Playable Verbs Before Theory

The game is experienced through Move, Search, Strike, Patrol / Screen, Support, Recover, Replenish, Hold. Command, information, friction, entropy, synchronization, deception, and logistics must modify those verbs rather than become separate mini-games.

VerbMain Decision
MoveWhere must I be, and how much exposure will I accept?
SearchHow much concealment or command attention will I trade for information?
StrikeAttack now, wait, synchronize, or conserve?
Patrol / ScreenWhat space or asset must I control before the enemy acts?
SupportWhere can I improve another action enough to matter?
RecoverIs restoring effectiveness worth yielding tempo?
ReplenishWhen do I leave the fight to restore staying power?
HoldWhen is patience more valuable than activity?

3. Detail Must Create Decisions

Keep detail when it changes player behavior. Useful detail includes range, signature, search posture, strike capacity, reaction posture, Endurance, command load, weapon commitment, synchronization timing, damage, and readiness. Be suspicious of detail that only proves fidelity.

5. Luck Reflects Decisions

The player should understand why an action is Poor, Even, Favorable, or Dominant before rolling. Dice determine where reality lands inside the decision-created band.

6. Physical Warfare Remains Tangible

Players maneuver task forces, establish screens, push submarines through chokepoints, launch strikes, defend, expend weapons, take damage, and fight over geography.

7. Time Is a Resource

Every Formation has a Ready Time. Actions, Friction, Recovery, Replenishment, and Synchronization alter when it may act again. The player should always care what the enemy can do before then.

8. Information Is Imperfect, Stale, and Contestable

Contacts track Location, Identity, and Age. Detection never permanently solves the information problem. Deception matters when it causes physical misallocation.

9. Command Attention Is Finite

Routine execution follows standing Missions. Command Slots are used for focused search, immediate retasking, synchronization, pushing through friction, restoring command, or forcing difficult action.

10. Entropy Is Core — Never Generic Morale

Entropy is capability that still exists physically but is temporarily unavailable.

SourceCauseEffect
FrictionHigh tempo / churn / complexityLonger action cycle
DisruptionEW / cyber / deception / command interruptionPoorer search / synchronization
DestructionHeavy damageReduced offensive capability

11. Cohesion Summarizes Entropy

Two sources = Disrupted. Three = Disorganized. The summary state says how well the Formation functions; the source boxes say why.

12. One Mechanical Language

Every subsystem should change one or more of: Time, Position, Information, Command, Entropy, Physical Capability.

13. The Sequence Teaches the Game

A new player should be able to follow the 10-step Sequence and answer: What do I know? What can I do? What is the reward? What is the risk? When can I act again?

14. Layers of Mastery

First game: Move, Search, Shoot, Defend. Fifth game: Signature, Reaction, Endurance, weapon conservation. Fifteenth game: Tempo, deception, command workload, synchronization, mission discipline.

15. Operational Skill, Not Stat Optimization

The better player should win mainly by managing uncertainty, timing, position, commitment, and risk.

16. Playtesting Is Part of the Design

Record obvious choices, repeated rule lookups, inactive-player downtime, unfair-feeling die results, unused actions, dominant actions, and outcomes players cannot explain.
